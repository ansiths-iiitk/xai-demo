In this hands-on you will not just look at explanations. You will test them. There are two investigations. Everything runs inside this page, on your phone or laptop. Nothing to install.

Investigation 1 · The leaf detective

Can an AI model correctly identify a leaf disease without looking at the leaf?

In the leaf-disease session, we trained a model on tomato-leaf photos taken in a lab. It did well on lab photos and badly on field photos. Here is one possible reason. Let us test it.

Step 1 · A model that is not allowed to see the leaf

The model below sees only the four small red squares in each corner of the photo. The leaf is hidden from it.

This model is already trained. Guess first: out of 100 lab photos, how many will it get right? Pure guessing among 4 diseases gives 25.

Step 2 · Test it on one photo

Tap a photo. The corner model answers using only the four corner squares.

Step 3 · Change only the background

Pick a lab photo above. Now paint its background with the background of a typical photo from another disease folder. The leaf stays exactly the same.

What this shows. The lab photos for each disease were taken in slightly different conditions: background, light, camera. A model can score well by learning those conditions instead of the disease. In the field, the background is soil and other leaves, so that shortcut stops working.
But: this shows the shortcut is in the photos. Did our leaf model (MobileNetV2) actually learn it? That needs a separate check on the model itself.

Investigation 2 · Why was this student flagged?

This is the same early-warning model used in the deployment session. It looks at attendance and internal marks and gives a chance that the student needs help. Above 50% the student is flagged for a call. (The 600 training students are made up for teaching.)

Step 1 · Choose a student

Why was this student flagged?

Show the technical details (contribution chart)

Start from a typical student (attendance 80%, marks 62). Each input pushes the model's risk score up (red) or down (grey). The black line is the 50% decision line.

Step 2 · Remove the flag, honestly

What is the smallest realistic change that takes this student below 50%? Move the slider and watch the model's answer. This kind of answer is called a counterfactual explanation.

Remember: this only changes the numbers given to the model. Raising a student's real attendance takes weeks of regular classes.
